# Summary: LCR MSCP Imperial Ponds Renovation Plan 2014.pdf

## Full Citation
Finnegan, A.D. 2014. Imperial Ponds Renovation Plan – 2014. Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program, Bureau of Reclamation, Lower Colorado Region, Boulder City, Nevada. April 2014.

---

## Overview

A detailed operational plan for chemically renovating all six IPCA ponds with rotenone in December 2014 – January 2015, prior to the 2017 restocking. This renovation was needed because previous renovation attempts (Ponds 1 and 3) had been incomplete, leaving nonnative fish in the system, and the original surface water supply had been identified as a nonnative fish invasion vector.

---

## Statement of Need

As of 2014, all six Imperial Ponds contained nonnative fish including:
- Black crappie (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*)
- Bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*)
- Common carp (*Cyprinus carpio*)
- Redear sunfish (*Lepomis microlophus*)
- Threadfin shad (*Dorosoma petenense*)
- Warmouth (*Lepomis gulosus*)
- Western mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*)

Previous renovations of Ponds 1 and 3 were unsuccessful, likely due to: (1) inadequate site preparation (vegetation removal), and (2) the screened surface water supply being a reinvasion vector. The water supply had since been converted to 100% well water, eliminating the main invasion pathway.

---

## Treatment Plan Actions
1. Remove BONY and RASU from Pond 1 (spring and autumn 2014) before renovation.
   - RASU to be transferred to A10 backwater (Ehrenberg, AZ); BONY to Lake Mead State Fish Hatchery.
2. Remove inundated vegetation from margins and hummocks of all five ponds and the Pond 5 marsh area.
3. Apply Prenfish® CFT Legumine™ (5% rotenone) to all six ponds.
4. Post-renovation monitoring targeting larval and adult fish.
5. Evaluate renovation success and identify failures.

---

## Key Methods

### Pre-Renovation
- **Native fish removal:** Trammel nets, hoop nets, trap nets used in Pond 1 (spring and fall 2014); population estimates between sampling events.
- **Vegetation removal (Oct 2014–Jan 2015):** Manual hedge trimmer removal of shoreline vegetation; Pond 5 marsh dewatered to elevation 180 ft, then burned (USFWS AZ Fire District) or manually cut. Post-burn, pond refilled to 185 ft.

### Rotenone Application
- Two-part treatment: first application December 2014; follow-up January 2015.
- Prior to application: ponds brought to elevation 185 ft to create positive head pressure and suppress groundwater intrusion (a freshwater lens that could dilute piscicide and shelter fish).
- Fish survival stations (hoop nets and minnow traps deployed night before) used to monitor kill effectiveness; monitored every 30 minutes.

### Application Rates
- Based on Prenfish® CFT Legumine™ guidance tables for lakes/ponds/reservoirs.
- Quantities calculated for each pond at water elevation 185 ft.

### Post-Renovation Monitoring
- Intensive monitoring for larval and adult fish following treatment.
- Longer-term post-renovation monitoring planned to confirm successful removal before any restocking.

---

## Relevance to BackwaterGenetics Project
- Explains the **gap in fish populations at IPCA prior to the 2017 stocking** — all fish were removed by this renovation and ponds were monitored before restocking.
- The 2014–2015 renovation is the direct precursor to the 2017 initial stocking event that begins the current monitoring record (IPCA data from 2016-01-01).
- Groundwater intrusion issues documented here (freshwater percolations from Colorado River) are relevant context for understanding water management challenges at the site.
- The transfer of Pond 1 fish to A10 backwater and Pond 1 BONY to LMSF Hatchery explains why some fish from earlier Pond 1 populations may appear in NFWG records.
