## Overview

Annual report for the Imperial Ponds Conservation Area (IPCA) covering FY2018. This is the first full year of monitoring following the 2017 initial stocking of all six ponds (the ponds had been renovated with rotenone in 2014–2015 and stocked in early 2017). The report covers water delivery, habitat management, native fish population monitoring, water quality, avian monitoring, habitat creation accomplishments, and adaptive management.

---

## Site Description
- IPCA: 126 acres within Imperial NWR, River Mile 59, Reach 5 of LCR MSCP.
- 6 disconnected backwaters totaling **80 acres** for RASU and BONY.
- 12 acres of managed marsh for California black rail, Yuma clapper rail, and western least bittern.
- 34 acres planned for cottonwood-willow riparian (not yet planted as of FY18).
- Water supplied from Imperial NWR groundwater wells (100% well water; no river connection).
- Land use agreement between Reclamation and USFWS signed 2006; site closed to the public.

---

## Water Delivery (FY2018)
- Target: ~8.5 ac-ft/month per pond (Jan–Jun, Oct–Dec); 17 ac-ft/month per pond (Jul–Sep); total ~771 ac-ft/year for all six ponds.
- Actual 2018 delivery: 738 ac-ft (close to target).

### FY18 Population Estimates (at September 2018; 21 months post-stocking)
- Pond 1 (RASU): ~180 fish (60% survival)
- Pond 3 (RASU): ~198 fish (66% survival)
- Pond 4 (RASU): ~198 fish (66% survival)
- BONY ponds (2, 5, 6): Population estimates also tracked via remote PIT scanning; mass spawning event observed in Pond 2 on April 20, 2018.

### Monitoring Method
- 2–3 permanent remote PIT scanners per pond, with weirs on one antenna in Ponds 4, 5, and 6 to maximize contacts.
- Data downloaded every 2–4 weeks.
- Population estimates calculated bi-weekly.
- Larval fish surveys, minnow trapping, and netting for recruitment monitoring.

### Recruitment
- Natural recruitment documented in all ponds during FY18.
- Mass BONY spawning event observed in Pond 2 (April 20, 2018): thousands to tens of thousands of fish in a 20-meter open-water stretch.

### Water Quality
- No FY18 water quality data reported (instrumentation and personnel issues); survival data suggest water quality remained within acceptable ranges.
