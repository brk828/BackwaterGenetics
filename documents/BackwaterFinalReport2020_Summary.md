## Overview

A genetics and demographic study examining persistence and reproductive success of PIT-tagged, stocked RASU in ephemeral and permanent off-channel ponds using microsatellite (14-locus) genotyping and remote PIT sensing. Study sites include:

- **AJ** — ephemeral pond (Arizona)
- **DAN** — ephemeral pond (likely Danson Wash area)
- **YUMA** — Yuma Cove Backwater (permanent pond)
- **Imperial ponds** (INWR) — covered in early-monitoring context (2016–2019)

PIT scanning data run through 2019 for Yuma and 2019 (June) for Imperial ponds.

---

## Key Methods
- 14 microsatellite loci for genetic characterization
- Parentage assignment via programs Mykiss and Colony
- Remote PIT scanning for individual detection/survival
- Remote sensing (aerial/satellite imagery) for site characterization
- Statistical analyses: regression of reproductive success vs. TL, number of mates

---

## Key Findings

### Genetic Variation
- No significant difference in variation between sexes or years/locations for stocked adults.
- More variation in adults than progeny; more variation in larvae than juveniles from the same cohort.
- This pattern likely reflects early-mortality effects reducing the number of contributing breeders.

### Parentage Assignment
- Average parentage success >86%, but highly variable.
- Ephemeral ponds: low success associated with small sample sizes.
- YUMA: reduced success linked to uncharacterized recruited/holdover individuals and relatedness — microsatellite markers insufficient; SNPs recommended.

### Post-Stocking Survival (Remote Sensing)
- Rapid loss of stocked individuals detected at all sites, **biased toward males** in both ephemeral and permanent ponds.
- Losses at YUMA particularly high → additional stockings in 2014 and 2015, but those stockings also showed dramatic losses (affecting females as well as males).
- Physical sampling at YUMA confirmed continuous recruitment of untagged individuals (recruits and holdovers).

### Reproductive Success
- Proportional contribution of males and females to reproduction was highly variable across locations and years (11.8–87.0% for females; 7.1–59.2% for males); no significant sex difference.
- Significant relationship between number of mates and offspring produced (similar to bonytail); no relationship between TL and reproductive success.
- High VRS likely driven by variable early mortality removing adults from the reproductive pool.
- At YUMA, examination of multi-year reproduction (2013–2015) showed: most surviving individuals contributed annually; some females skipped years; some fish lost to PIT scanning still assigned offspring (tag loss/expulsion suspected).

### YUMA-Specific Results
- Parentage assignments 2013 were comparable to ephemeral ponds; became more difficult in 2014–2015 due to recruited fish with shared ancestry.
- Natural recruitment and reproduction confirmed in YUMA.

### Remote Sensing
- Used to assess habitat structure and changes at ephemeral pond sites.

## Relevance to BackwaterGenetics Project
- **Predecessor to the current project**: This report established the baseline demographic and genetic patterns at YCB and introduced the use of PIT scanning and parentage genetics as monitoring tools.
- Explains the early post-stocking sex-biased survival pattern at Yuma that is now well documented through 2025.
- The move from microsatellites (14 loci) to GT-seq SNPs addressed limitations identified in this report.
- Ephemeral ponds (AJ, DAN) are **not** part of the current BackwaterGenetics project; current project covers only YCB (LID 592) and IPCA ponds (LIDs 1043–1048).
- The 2020 draft is a precursor to the 2025 Final Report (`2025FinalReport.docx`).
