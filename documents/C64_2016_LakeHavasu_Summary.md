# Summary: Distribution and Post-Stocking Survival of Bonytail in Lake Havasu, 2013–2016

**Source:** `documents/C64_2016_Lake Havasu Final Project Report.docx`. Humphrey, K.G., B.R. Kesner, and P.C. Marsh. 2016. Report to the Bureau of Reclamation by Marsh & Associates, LLC, Tempe, Arizona. LCR MSCP Reach 3 (Lake Havasu, open water/mainstem).

## What it is
A 3-year (2013–2016) acoustic/radio telemetry (6 iterations) and remote PIT-scanning (5 iterations) study of post-stocking bonytail (BONY) fate and habitat use in Lake Havasu (mainstem Colorado River reservoir, Reach 3), at four sites: Bill Williams River NWR, Blankenship Bend, Regional Park Moabi, and Laughlin Lagoon. Unlike the off-channel (OCH) ponds this project (`BackwaterGenetics`) covers, all four sites are open-water / river-connected, with resident nonnative predator communities (largemouth bass, striped bass, flathead catfish, piscivorous birds).

## Headline result: near-total, rapid post-release loss
Of 85 telemetry-tagged bonytail across the study, 44 were confirmed mortalities and 35 were permanently lost to the study (never contacted again). Combining confirmed mortalities and lost fish, overall apparent mortality reached an estimated 55% at Blankenship Bend, 93% at Regional Park Moabi, and 100% at Laughlin Lagoon. With the exception of Laughlin Lagoon (where loss/mortality occurred more gradually, not less completely), most telemetry fish were lost or confirmed dead within a month of release. PIT scanning data independently corroborate this: most detections across all four sites occurred within the first one to two weeks post-stocking, then declined steeply — at least a 90% drop in contacts from week 1 to week 2 at most sites.

## Cause of mortality: predation, not size, dominates
- **Avian predation** is identified as a major and possibly dominant cause. The report states predation was a major threat at all study areas and that piscivorous birds likely accounted for a large proportion of telemetry-tagged mortality, supported by tag recoveries under known cormorant/heron roost sites and a direct observation of a great blue heron capturing a bonytail.
- **Fish predation** is also directly documented: a stocked bonytail was recovered from the digestive tract of a largemouth bass, and striped bass and flathead catfish are implicated by movement-pattern evidence (rapid, atypical displacement preceding a "mortality" determination).
- **Size at release** is discussed as a plausible but confounded factor, explicitly imported from other species' literature: the report notes that total length at release is a known determinant of post-stocking survival in razorback sucker and other species and "may be a factor in bonytail survival as well" — stated as a hypothesis, not an established bonytail finding from this study, since site/season/turbidity/rearing-history differences confound any size effect the authors could isolate. Even where larger fish were used (Laughlin Lagoon), the ultimate outcome was no better (up to 100% loss/mortality), though those fish were tracked longer before that outcome — a slower decline, not a lower ceiling.

## Mark-recapture modeling was attempted and failed
The authors explicitly tried to fit the continuous PIT-scanning data to Cormack-Jolly-Seber and Barker mark-recapture models and could not obtain usable estimates: a "complex and biologically realistic model with time varying survival and re-sight rates resulted in highly variable parameter estimates for survival and re-sight rates (nearly 0 to 1)" even among structural models with acceptable likelihood values. This is a direct, on-the-record precedent for the open-water component of any future bonytail IPM: even a survival-only model with realistic time-varying structure was not identifiable from Reach 3's own telemetry + PIT data.

## Long-term program context
Of roughly 55,000 PIT-tagged bonytail stocked into Lake Havasu from 1993–2014, only 80 were ever recaptured during routine monitoring, and only 3 of those more than a year after release. Of ~25,000 stocked with scannable tags since 2011, 1,832 were contacted by PIT scanning, but only 6 more than a year post-stocking and only 175 at large more than 30 days.

## Habitat / behavior notes
Bonytail showed limited dispersal from release sites, nocturnal activity, and some use of bulrush/riprap cover where available; sites with a single constricted river connection (Regional Park Moabi, Laughlin Lagoon) were better suited to tracking because fish did not egress, but this did not translate into materially better survival outcomes.

## Recommendations (as stated)
Continue PIT tagging, increase study-fish size, stock into predator-free backwaters wherever available, coordinate hatchery timing for larger fish, continue trammel-net monitoring, and consider stocking-schedule timing relative to migratory avian predator abundance.
