Conducted in Cooperation with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service for Bureau of Reclamation, Lower Colorado Region. Boulder City, NV. July 11, 2005.

## Overview

This report documents the outcome of a December 1, 2004 design workshop convened to redesign the existing "DU2 Ponds" at the Imperial National Wildlife Refuge (INWR) into optimized backwater habitat for native Lower Colorado River fishes. The redesigned facility became what is now the Imperial Ponds Conservation Area (IPCA).

---

## Background: Why the Original DU2 Ponds Failed

The DU2 Ponds were originally constructed in autumn 2002 under the 1997 Biological Opinion as part of a requirement for Reclamation to create ≥300 acres of protected native fish habitat. After initial stocking with ~658 fingerling RASU in March 2003, the population declined to ~25 fish while nonnative warmouth sunfish (Lepomis gulosus) reached ~17,000.

Two key failure modes were identified:
1. **Hypoxia** — single inflow/outflow design, inability to maintain target water surface elevation (due to hydrologic connection with adjacent farm fields), and resulting aquatic macrophyte overgrowth.
2. **Nonnative fish persistence** — despite rotenone treatments, warmouth persisted; design did not allow adequate pond isolation.

---

## Workshop Objective
Establish design criteria and develop a preferred design (with costs) to both improve habitat functions and increase habitat acreage within the existing 96-acre site.

## Existing Conditions at the Time of Workshop
- 6 ponds ranging from 4–14 acres; total ~35 acres surface area; max depth 6 feet.
- Single inflow/outflow per pond; water flows north-to-south through the system.
- Inlet screens prevent fish >1 inch from entering/exiting.
- Heavy cattail, common reed, and bulrush; sandy-silt substrate; dense submerged macrophytes in spring/summer.
- ~50 acres of upland originally designated for riparian forest, largely overrun by saltcedar and mesquite.

---

## Design Goals (Prioritized)
1. **(Highest)** Reconstruct ponds to meet physical, chemical, and biological conditions supporting native LCR fishes in a healthy condition.
2. **(Medium)** Expand surface area and/or marsh habitat within the 96-acre site; design easily maintainable features with a 50-year target life.
3. **(Lower)** Design system to increase knowledge of managed habitat for LCR fishes.

Key design decision: Ponds would **not** be connected to the river (unlike Beal Lake or Cibola HLP). Water supply = groundwater wells and/or pumped river water only, to eliminate nonnative fish invasion pathways.

---

## Design Features
- Six disconnected ponds (Ponds 1–6) with individual plan views provided.
- Hummocks and structural complexity added to improve habitat heterogeneity.
- Improved water control infrastructure with multiple inflow/outflow points per pond.
- 100% well-water supply assumed in later plans to eliminate nonnative fish vectors.
