# Summary: BONY Proposed IPM — Model Description (9-2-26)

**Source:** `documents/BONY Proposed IPM - Model Description 9-2-26.docx`. Sound Science LLC, LCR MSCP Native Fish Data Synthesis Phase 3, Sub-Task 2 (Bonytail Demographic Analysis). Draft for advisory panel review, 10 July 2026.

## What it is
A proposed integrated population model (IPM) for bonytail (BONY) across the LCR MSCP program, combining (1) an off-channel (OCH) stage-structured state-space IPM for predator-free conservation ponds — Cibola High Levee Pond (CHLP), Imperial Ponds Conservation Area (IPCA), Yuma Cove, and ephemeral backwaters "where data support" — and (2) an open-water Cormack–Jolly–Seber (CJS) survival-only model for mainstem releases, where post-release survival is near zero within weeks and there is no persistent population to track.

## Life cycle / stages
Four stages collapsed to an annual time step: newly-stocked adult → established adult, with early wild stages (S1–2, S2–4) and reproduction (F) closing the loop only in isolated ponds. Stocking enters as a known input, not an estimated rate.

## Model as a DAG (three joined sub-models)
- **Reproduction (gold):** F from genetic parentage of larvae/YOY.
- **OCH abundance (grey, state-space):** stage abundances N³/N⁴ evolve under survival + known stocking, generating PIT-scan and net counts; observation error σ scales counts to true abundance.
- **Encounter (green, CJS):** PIT histories (ponds + open water) generated by φ³/φ⁴ and detection p.

φ³/φ⁴ sit in the overlap of the abundance and encounter sub-models — the defining IPM joint-likelihood integration. Abundance is capture-recapture based (open-population Jolly-Seber-style), not a raw census: PIT scanning and netting enter as two detection methods on the same latent N, and the netted untagged fraction scales tagged abundance to pond total (assumes equal catchability; falls back to within-season closed-population recapture if not). Close-kin mark-recapture from the same genetic panel is noted as a possible future extension (parent-offspring now feasible; half-sibling would need a denser SNP panel), not part of the current model.

## Site coverage
Only IP Ponds 2, 5, 6 and CHLP have enough survey effort/recapture to be modeled. Explicitly dropped: IP Ponds 1 and 3 and Yuma Cove backwater (recapture never exceeds 5.3%), pre-2016 Pond 2 records (~800 fish stocked 2007, never redetected), and CHLP records from 2016 onward (population effectively untagged, closure violated).

## Reproduction (F) estimation
F = spawning participation × per-capita reproductive success, estimated from parentage assignment of genotyped larvae/YOY against a near-complete genotyped adult pool (no stock-recruit function or females-only proxy needed). Cites Osborne et al. (2018): participation 89–99% in good years, collapsing to 27–33% in a poor year (a different pond system, "Nine Mile," 2016) — used as evidence that participation and reproductive skew are environmentally driven. Absolute F magnitude is pinned by trammel-net recruit counts; parentage supplies only the relative apportionment across parents.

## Covariates
`log F`, `logit φ⁴` ~ environmental covariates (dissolved oxygen, temperature, water level) + pond-specific intercept + year random effect. `logit φ³` ~ release/habitat covariates (reach, size at release, method, predator load, timing, site). Covariate slopes are shared across ponds (pooled), intercepts are pond-specific; no hierarchical pooling of pond-level rates themselves (too few ponds to identify a hyper-SD). Sex is deliberately **not** used as a survival covariate (unreliable field assignment, confounded with release size), though the doc notes pond experiments elsewhere found lower survival for stocked males than females (citing Marsh et al. 2024).

## Key assumptions/caveats stated in the document
- Open water has no persistent population (near-zero but not exactly-zero annual survival, kept estimable rather than fixed at 0).
- OCH ponds are treated as effectively predator-free with demographic continuity; the 2020 (IPCA/IP5) and 2022 (IPCA/IP2) die-offs are attributed to well pump failure (operational, not environmental) per Marsh et al. (2024), and a CHLP die-off in the early 2000s to an illicit largemouth bass introduction — both carried as one-off shocks via the year random effect, not covariate signal.
- Requires clean, de-duplicated, individual-level PIT histories.

## Modeling progression
Presented as a staged build: open-water CJS → off-channel CJS with pond fixed effects → covariate slopes on survival → full state-space abundance + recruitment integration in ponds where reproduction closes the loop. Each site/rung is only as complex as its data justify.
